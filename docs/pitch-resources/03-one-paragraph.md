# One paragraph

CryptoSure is privacy-preserving insurance for crypto held in wallets, built on Midnight. It writes defined, provable coverage in two forms: a periodic wallet policy and point-of-use protection for a single high-value transfer, the moment of highest risk. The first instrument, the WalletBinder, is a single Compact contract (compiled on compactc 0.35.0 with full proving keys) that gates a wallet onto a policy only after three checks pass inside one ZK (zero-knowledge) proof: the wallet is clear of sanctions denylists at a fresh list version, the holder has passed KYC (Know Your Customer, regulated identity verification) at the required level with an approved issuer, and the holder lives in a jurisdiction the program is licensed for. Each check is a Merkle membership proof, so the chain learns that some eligible holder bound some cleared wallet and nothing else; identity, location, and the raw wallet address never land on-chain. CryptoSure consumes KYCz and GeoZ, two DIDz-family instruments, as sealed black boxes: a trusted issuer verifies the person, seals the result, and holds the trap door, so lawful access exists through a warrant served on the issuer while the insurer holds nothing that could leak. Coverage then flows through tiered education, a signed contract, a human underwriting review, and a policy with a set amount, set period, and clearly written scope. The Build Club demo runs on simulated KYC and sanctions data so the gate can be exercised end to end; the contracts do not change when real feeds replace the fixtures. CryptoSure is the verifier in the cohort's one-issuer, one-credential, one-verifier pattern, and the thesis behind it is simple: crypto is uniquely insurable, because every loss leaves a permanent trail and ownership can be proven cryptographically, and nobody has built the insurance market for it yet.
